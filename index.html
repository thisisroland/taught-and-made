About this archive

This is a working archive of final projects made by students I have taught, in university studios and in secondary classrooms. Each project appears the way the student presented it: the research boards, the iterations that failed, the finished artefact, and the room where the work met an audience.

Nothing has been redesigned after the fact. The boards are the students’ own documents, reproduced intact, because the thinking visible on them is the part worth showing. What I contribute is the brief, the pressure, and the questions that sent each of them back to the drawing table.

  1. Investigating

    Research, precedent, and the problem as it was actually found.

  2. Developing

    Concepts tested, narrowed, and argued on paper.

  3. Creating & Making

    Prototypes, fabrication, failure, and the finished artefact.

  4. Evaluating

    Audience response, survey data, and what the student would change.

01

Visual Making Project

Course Exhibition

Mixed media, group show

  • Exhibition
  • SDG-led brief

Every piece in this studio starts from an issue rather than from a material. Students are asked to find something happening in society or in the environment, locate it inside the UN Sustainable Development Goals, and then argue it in three dimensions instead of on a page. The object has to carry the argument across a room, before anyone reads the label.

The work on these tables reaches across several of those goals. Fertiliser runoff from oil palm feeding the algae blooms that suffocate fish, and the corporate framing that calls it growth, sit under responsible consumption and life below water. Coral bleaching rendered as a screen asking whether the reef is still blue enough for your feed belongs to climate action. The illegal wildlife trade appears more than once: an elephant resting on an open book whose story has been silenced, orangutans waiting behind a theatre curtain, a reptile skin dressed as a garment worn once and gone forever. A claw machine full of plush toys, warmly lit and entirely friendly, is about how predators choose and approach children, which is where peace, justice and strong institutions stop being an abstraction.

Showing the work in a real room changes it again. Students have to decide how a piece is approached, how far back a viewer stands, and what a stranger needs to be told before the work can speak for itself. The two projects that follow came out of this studio.

Hidden Spotlight

Art Installation

Laura Noshita Faiz

  • Visual Making Project
  • 58 × 43 × 34 cm

Hidden Spotlight is a carnival tent about the size of a table, and it does not want to be enjoyed. Lit from inside, the painted ringmaster on its panels throws a shadow onto the wall behind, and the shadow has an animal’s head. The lettering around the roof asks the question outright: buta mata atau nurani. Blind eyes, or blind conscience.

The subject is the illegal wildlife trade and the use of animals as entertainment. Laura researched it through JAAN, the Jakarta Animal Aid Network, and benchmarked the form against Joseph Cornell’s assemblage boxes before building anything. Her workbook includes a sensory translation table, mass and surface and transparency and space, that converts the feeling she wanted into material decisions: wooden frame, tracing paper, mica, one internal lamp.

The honest part is the middle. The first structure was too flimsy, the paper too bright and the shadows too soft, and the revision pages show each of those being corrected rather than quietly dropped.

Angelpod

Product Design

Karenina Kirsten Kwok

  • Self-defence device
  • Film

Angelpod is a self-defence device for women, developed out of Indonesian violence statistics and shaped around how a hand actually grips under stress. The project board cites 25,050 cases of violence against women out of 27,589 recorded cases in Indonesia in 2022, and uses that as the design brief rather than as decoration.

The body is hard silicone over stainless steel, roughly 10 cm tall and 3.5 cm across, holding a pepper spray, an emergency alarm and a foldable pocket knife. Colour is assigned meaning on the board, courage and power and femininity, and the form was resolved through repeated hand-grip studies. The documentation ends where it should: a survey asking respondents whether the finished object does what the research said it would.

321 Scope

Interior and Architecture

Kenza Madeline

  • Care centre
  • Film

A care centre for children with Down syndrome, aged five to twelve, designed around human-centred design and sensory stimulus theory. The name carries the argument: 321 for the third copy of the twenty-first chromosome, and also as a countdown to something good; scope for coverage, for taking the child in rather than setting them apart.

In Indonesia, there are schools and special therapy centers for people with Down syndrome, but there is a lack of specialized care centers designed with appropriate architectural and interior designs to support their developmental process. The 321 Scope care center aims to bridge this gap by creating a space that combines functionality, comfort, and aesthetics to provide a suitable environment for children with Down syndrome to grow and thrive.

Project statement, Kenza Madeline

The documentation runs the full studio sequence: background and theoretical basis, design method, identity, a colour palette assigned by psychological effect, floor plans and artist drawings, 3D modelling, rendering, and a closing evaluation.

Batari

Cutting Paper Art

Valencia Geraldine

  • Visual narrative
  • Film

Batari retells the Indonesian folk tale Awang Sukma dan Telaga Bidadari as a layered paper-cut scene. Each plane was illustrated separately, cut, and stacked inside a lit box so the image gains real depth instead of drawn depth.

The method section is unusually disciplined for school work: logo, colour, typography and paper cutting are each argued as separate decisions, then tested against readers. The evaluation figures on the final board sit above ninety per cent across every question asked.

Next project07 — Wingko Babad

Wingko Babad

Packaging Redesign

Prinvia Prichariel

  • Brand strategy
  • Film

Wingko Babad Cap Kereta Api is a Semarang institution: a coconut and glutinous rice cake sold out of the railway station since 1946, bought as the thing you bring home. The cake was never the problem. The packaging was. It did not keep the product fresh, the design drifted between print runs, and it looked too cheap for the gift shelf it was made for.

Prinvia rebuilt it from the brand outward. Seventy-five pages run before a single sketch appears: distribution network, competitor set, positioning map, brand loyalty, packaging trends, a model-view analysis scored against visibility, information, emotional appeal and workability. Only then do three problems get named, and one idea answer them.

Otentik rasanya, nostalgia terasa bersama. Authentic in taste, nostalgia tasted together.

Big idea, Wingko Babad design deck

The primary pack holds five flavours in a structure strong enough to stack and to open without tearing. The secondary pack is designed to be handed over, which is what this product is actually for. Then comes the part most student projects skip: Prinvia took the finished pack to the exam exhibition and asked strangers, students from other programmes and other semesters, to handle it and say what they thought. The conclusions on the last pages are theirs, not hers.

Pasola Festival

Book Layout Design

Caroline Adonia Phe

  • Editorial

Pasola is a ritual jousting festival on Sumba, in East Nusa Tenggara: horsemen from rival villages riding at each other with wooden spears to open the planting season. Caroline set out to explain it to readers between fifteen and twenty-six who had most likely never heard of it.

The logbook is a design document in the strict sense. It opens with research and pictorial sources, builds a user persona, then works through mindmap, moodboard, key visuals, typography and colour before a single page is laid out. The second half is production: illustration assets drawn one at a time, a stated photography grading formula, flatplan, layout grid, thumbnails, and then the book itself, digital first and then printed and photographed in the hand.

Next project09 — Pasola Festival

Pasola Festival

Website and Mobile

Caroline Adonia Phe

  • UI design
  • Responsive
  • Film

The same subject carried into a different medium. Where the book asks for a reader who will sit still, a website has to survive someone arriving from a search result on a phone, halfway through something else.

Caroline rebuilt the Pasola material as a responsive site: sitemap, user flowchart, high-fidelity wireframes drawn for mobile first, and then a design system of its own. A six-colour palette, a working icon set, article card templates, and illustration assets drawn one instrument at a time, the jungga and the sasando and the gong and the tifa.

The evaluation is real rather than decorative. Fourteen respondents filled in a form over four days in December 2024, and the logbook reports what they found easy to follow, where the prototype’s buttons were too generous, and what they asked for more of.

Next project10 — Cow Play Cow Moo
10

Cow Play Cow Moo

Re-Branding Design

Joanna Renardi

  • Identity system
  • 88-page brand book
  • Film

Cow Play Cow Moo is a Singaporean family arcade chain. Joanna’s brief was a rebrand, and she treated it as a positioning problem before a drawing problem: market presence, onsite observation, a point-of-difference matrix, a positioning map against competitors, and a stated argument for why the old identity was holding the business back.

The idea she landed on is a pun that happens to also be a strategy. Endless MOON-ments: the arcade as a universe you step into rather than a room you visit. The wordmark folds the four letters into a compact monogram with a cow’s face hiding inside it, and the system grows outward from there, into supergraphics, pictograms, a staff uniform, a motion logo, and an eighty-eight page brand book that closes with its own invitation.

The last pages record the part that matters most. The work was shown at the BINUS Alam Sutera atrium in December 2025, and the interview feedback she collected there is transcribed rather than summarised.

The Absurd Parade

Poster

Andina Rahmi Awalya

  • Visual Making Project
  • Film

Andina’s workbook is set inside a circus tent, and the tent is the argument rather than the decoration. She studied Surrealism through Dalí, Magritte, Escher, Picabia and Duchamp, and Dadaism through Man Ray and Duchamp again, sketching from each work before writing down what it was doing. The two movements meet in her big idea: an absurd parade, somewhere between control and chaos.

Two posters came out of it. One paints a clown releasing a balloon that reads CREATIVITY; the other is a cut-paper collage assembled from circus debris, a penny-farthing and a drum and a star and scattered type. Both carry the same borrowed line about creativity coming out of a conflict of ideas, and the workbook argues its way there instead of starting from it.

Design of Society

Brand and Social Design

Five student brands

  • Public exhibition

Design of Society asks a student to build a brand that answers a real social need, and then to sell it in public. Each one ran the whole arc alone, survey and positioning and identity and product and packaging and marketing and evaluation, and then staged a booth at the exhibition, where visitors became the test audience rather than a jury.

What makes the booths worth documenting is that they are not mock-ups. The aprons were worn, the packaging was handled, and the survey data on the evaluation boards came from the people standing in front of the table.

The cohort01

grdn bench

A public bench for a college campus, drawn from the observation that students choose to sit outdoors and then have nowhere comfortable to do it. The form is a ribbed curve of teak on a stainless steel frame, chosen to survive weather and constant use. Survey data, a scale model, billboard and banner mock-ups and a closing evaluation complete the case.

Nightosphere

Game Design

Richard Gabriel

  • Unity
  • Film

A dark fantasy platformer, built in Unity and documented as a workbook rather than a pitch. The sequence is honest about where game art actually starts: rough character sketches on paper, then a sprite sheet, then a level background painted as one continuous drawing before it is ever cut into tiles.

The state screens carry the tone, a main menu and a death screen and a stage-cleared screen, and the final boards show the Unity scene itself, which is where all of it has to survive contact with the engine.

Seashell Lamp

Digital Fabrication

One brief, two processes

  • Laser cutting
  • 3D printing

The same seashell was resolved twice, once cut flat and assembled from ribs, once printed as a single volume. The two sets of documentation are worth reading against each other, because the process changes the question. Flat-pack asks how the parts hold together; printing asks whether the shape can stand at all.

Laser cutting

A desk lamp cut flat and assembled into a shell. The mood board is literal, conch and scallop, and the lamp keeps their ribbing and their curl without turning into pastiche. The failures are kept in the record: the components were too small to lift off the laser bed and had to be moved with masking tape, and the finished parts kept springing apart until the locking tabs were glued.
